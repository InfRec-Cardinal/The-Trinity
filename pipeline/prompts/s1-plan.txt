# Stage 1 - Plan focused search areas

Plan searches for published multimodal-agent tasks under the shared input policy. Preserve text, image and video protocols; only audio task inputs are excluded. The primary research focus is actual interactive games, puzzles and simulated worlds; web, operating-system and document interaction should remain present as a small support sample. Choose the search regions and eventual task mechanisms yourself. No named benchmark, fixed task list or taxonomy is prescribed.

Use about 9-15 compact areas when the maximum allows it, without exhausting the maximum as a target. Give at least 6 and no more than maximumAreas. At least two thirds of areas must have researchRole: game_world. The remaining areas have researchRole: support. These labels allocate research effort; they are not classification directions. Do not inflate the game count by splitting instances of one mechanism or relabeling static questions and office tasks as games.

Every game_world area must target actual interactive objectives, rules, observations, actions, feedback and terminal scoring. Choose distinct mechanisms rather than a catalogue of popular task names. Optional cues include rule discovery, physical interaction, control/timing, resources/construction, partial-observation navigation, adversarial/cooperative decisions and active perception. Combine or extend them as useful; none is a compulsory category. Explain which mechanism difference warrants an independent search.

Include exactly one combined support area with supportDomain: digital_interaction for web, operating-system and document interaction together. Do not allocate separate browser, desktop, mobile-GUI and document areas merely because they use different applications. Other support areas use supportDomain: other; game_world areas use supportDomain: null. Each support area may record at most three distinct protocols. There is no required number of game tasks or searches.

Search over the five types of source: research benchmarks released with code or data; public AI competitions; human contests and testing standards; published games and puzzle communities; and professional practice (task manuals, rulebooks, inspection or testing protocols). Original author/organizer material, forum task/result discussions and source implementations are traceable sources within these types. Many relevant tasks do not call themselves an AI or multimodal benchmark. Write queries using the field's own rule, objective, action and scoring vocabulary. Prefer genuinely different mechanisms over many entries from the same suite or community.

The later pipeline records whether a benchmark has an interactive protocol (permitted observations, public actions and a verifiable terminal outcome) or a fixed protocol (one scored answer/artifact per item and an official evaluator). Both use the same capability-based classification and train/unseen split requirements. Never select protocols that require audio input; video, prerecorded clips and screenshots returned after environment actions are all eligible. Interface availability is examined later; a published playable protocol without a usable official interface may still support faithful reconstruction.

For every area give id, title, why, queries, researchRole and supportDomain. Use a unique 2-41 character ASCII kebab-case id, beginning with a lowercase letter. Put descriptive details in the other fields. If an identifier is rejected, repair it without dropping its intended coverage. If the allocation is rejected, revise the actual search regions and roles instead of merely changing labels. Submit exactly one plan_search_areas call.
