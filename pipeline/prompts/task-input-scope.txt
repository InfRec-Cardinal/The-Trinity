# Shared task policy: exclude audio inputs

Apply this input policy to every library task, both task-tree roots and every node validation task. Prerecorded video and video-understanding protocols are eligible. Exclude tasks that require audio input.

Research tasks for evaluating and improving a multimodal agent. Choose what to search, which sources to follow, which task protocols qualify and how to classify them yourself. There is no required benchmark, task family or direction list. Public benchmarks, human competitions, forum task/result discussions and other traceable public sources are available source options, not mandatory coverage categories.

The current research focus is interactive games, puzzles and simulated worlds. These must have an actual objective, permitted observations, public actions, rule-governed consequences and an externally verifiable outcome. A fixed question set or ordinary web/office workflow is not a game merely because its name or presentation suggests one. Allocate most discovery effort to distinct game/world mechanisms; keep web, operating-system and document interaction as a small combined support sample. Other eligible text, code, visual and video tasks may remain as supporting coverage. Research allocation is not a preset taxonomy or an admission quota: never fabricate game tasks, relabel support tasks, weaken evidence checks or discard qualified results merely to meet a ratio.

Optional broad mechanism cues include rule discovery, planning under partial observations, physical interaction, control and timing, resource use, construction, navigation, adversarial or cooperative decisions, and active perception. Computer vision, video understanding, embodied interaction, tool/API use, coding/software work and reasoning may also inform supporting coverage. Choose, combine or extend these cues yourself; they are neither a required task inventory nor the final directions. Grow the taxonomy from reviewed task mechanisms.

- Preserve published text tasks and RGB-image/screenshot tasks. Text may be instructions, questions, answer options, documents, code or the environment observation itself. Pure text tasks remain eligible. Other input interfaces are not excluded by this policy.
- Interactive tasks may supply a new screenshot, video segment or textual/structured observation after each action. Record the actual observation protocol rather than assuming every interactive task has RGB observations.
- **Video is eligible.** Prerecorded clips, streamed video, frames sampled from a clip and video-understanding question sets are all admissible task inputs. Record them with the canonical `video` channel so the actual protocol stays visible.
- Exclude audio, speech and music task inputs. A task whose Player must listen to a soundtrack, a spoken question or a sound cue is out of scope.
- A video whose soundtrack carries information the task requires is an audio task, not a video task. Admit such a suite only through a separately identifiable, published subset whose items are answerable from the visual stream alone, with evidenced rules, reproducible item selection and an official scorer. Do not transcribe, mute or otherwise strip a required audio channel to make a protocol eligible.
- Preserve the published information constraints: expose only observations the original task permits. Text, DOM/accessibility trees, structured state, depth and point clouds are not automatically excluded; hidden answers or privileged state remain forbidden when the original protocol withholds them.
- Source-document illustrations or videos used to research a task are distinct from inputs the Player must consume during that task.

**Benchmarks** include interactive tasks and fixed, published evaluations. Interactive protocols expose permitted observations, public actions, resulting feedback and a verifiable terminal outcome. Fixed protocols provide a question or material and assess one final answer or artifact with an official evaluator; internal reasoning and tool use may span multiple steps. A fixed video question remains a benchmark even when no action changes the clip.

Both kinds enter the same requirement-based task tree and the same train/unseen split. Task kind describes the execution interface only: it never exempts a benchmark from splitting, capability analysis or evaluation. Every task needs source-supported rules, permitted inputs and externally verifiable scoring. A usable official interactive route must expose the actual observation/control protocol and documented trajectory recording. Otherwise use Real2Sim only when a faithful reconstruction preserves the evidenced mechanism and information constraints. Fixed evaluations retain their official scorer and record the permitted observations, submitted answer or artifact, and outcome. Reputation alone never establishes eligibility.

For a published specification, submit `inputScope` with all actual observation/input `channels`, the actual `textRole` and captured `evidence` establishing that protocol. Use canonical `audio` for required speech/music/sound input and `video` for prerecorded clips, streamed video or sampled clip frames. For other channels use stable, descriptive ids such as `rgb`, `text`, `depth`, `point_cloud`, `structured_state` or a more specific sensor/interface id; this is not a closed list. Only audio channels are excluded; a nonempty supported set of other channels is eligible. Independently review that evidence and the original observation mechanism, including a required soundtrack disguised as a video-only protocol. Unsupported scope is insufficient, not automatic admission. Newly designed validation tasks may have no source quotation for their new interface, but must describe it explicitly and pass independent input-scope review.
