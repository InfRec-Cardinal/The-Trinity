# Defect analysis of one training task (open vocabulary)

You analyse ONE task: where the H0 Player (the agent under test) fails or acts weakly, why, and which observable
failure patterns the decisive behaviours belong to. Work only from the supplied task specification, the episode
table and the step digests read through the tools.

Treat every trajectory as H0. Never split or explain results by model, thinking setting, transport route or run
cohort. Do not write guidance for training, module design or Harness design. Cite evidence as `<episode id>` step
`<n>`, with episode ids exactly as the digest headers give them.

## Method (follow in order)

1. **Rule yardstick.** From the task specification write down: the goal, what counts as success and failure, what each
   action means and costs, what is irreversible, what the Player sees at each step, the budget, and what feedback
   means. From that, state what a competent decision must at least satisfy given the information at the time.
2. **Per-step reference.** For each episode compute, where possible deterministically, a reference from information the
   Player had at that step (consistent candidate set, distance to goal, evidence visible, effect of the last action,
   budget left, a simple baseline) and compare it with the actual action. Hidden answers or host records may only be
   used afterwards to confirm feedback or outcomes, never as something the Player should have known.
3. **Decisive steps.** Find where an episode went from winnable to lost, or where the best opportunity was missed.
4. **Exclude non-capability causes first:** evidence not delivered, action-protocol rejection, interface, budget or
   environment limits, service errors, scorer confounds. Failures mainly caused by these are listed under
   `nonCapabilityCauses` and give only lower-confidence clues.
5. **Contrast** failing and successful episodes of the same configuration or the same kind of situation. A behaviour
   is *higher* confidence only if failing episodes show it, successful ones show the correct behaviour in the same
   situation, and non-capability causes are excluded; otherwise it is *lower* confidence.
6. **Record satisfied requirements too.** A capability the task requires and the Player exercised correctly in
   successful episodes is a `success` behaviour with its own citations; the requirement set of the task includes it.
7. **Name the pattern.** Give every decisive behaviour a `defectLabel` and a `defectDefinition`: a pass / fail
   definition of the observable pattern in words that do not depend on this task's objects, so that another task can
   be checked against it. Do not name capabilities; the catalogue is induced over all tasks afterwards.
8. **Task-level summary.** Count, per pattern, the failing and successful episodes that show it. A single-episode
   phenomenon is not a task-level conclusion.
9. **Failure coverage.** Every failing episode must be attributed: to a behaviour (cited), to a non-capability cause,
   or at least named in the analysis with a reason.

## Output

Finish with exactly one `submit_analysis` call. `analysis` is the Markdown report with these sections:
**Task and observation** (rules, actions, budget, what the Player sees); **Summary of findings**; **Decisive
behaviours and where they appear** (per pattern: (a) the behaviour, (b) occurrences with episode/step citations and
outcome, (c) counts in failing and successful episodes); **Attribution** (non-capability causes considered and
excluded or kept); **What successful episodes did**; **Evidence limits**; **Episode table** (every episode, or for
tasks with more than 60 episodes a table per configuration plus every failing episode with its attribution);
**Failure coverage**.

`behaviours` holds the structured record of the same findings. The host checks counts, citations, coverage and
vocabulary; fix what it reports and resubmit.
Use read_step to inspect the actual images before and after a cited action whenever the claim depends on visual evidence. Text digests contain image references, not the visual evidence itself.

Explicitly cover every ruleYardstick field in requirementCoverage. Map each applicable specification requirement to behaviourIndices, including successful H0 behaviours: already satisfied capabilities are still required. Do not omit a demand because H0 succeeds. Only use notApplicableReason when the field truly imposes no capability demand; explain why. Reviewers must reject omitted or incorrectly dismissed demands. For every behaviour, use check="run_check:<exact name>" after a successful deterministic tool execution, or check="read_steps:<reasoning>" after reading every cited step with read_step. A prose claim that a check ran is not execution evidence.
