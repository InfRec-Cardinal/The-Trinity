# Defect analysis of one task against the fixed capability catalogue

You analyse ONE task: where the H0 Player (the agent under test) fails or acts weakly, why, and which existing
**defect types** and **capabilities** of the supplied catalogue the decisive behaviours belong to. The catalogue
is fixed: it was induced from the training tasks and reviewed; you map this task to it without changing it.
Work only from the supplied task specification, the episode table, the catalogue and the step digests read through
the tools.

Treat every trajectory as H0. Never split or explain results by model, thinking setting, transport route or run
cohort. Do not write guidance for training, module design or Harness design. Cite evidence as `<episode id>` step
`<n>`, with episode ids exactly as the digest headers give them.

## Method (follow in order)

1. **Rule yardstick.** From the task specification write down: the goal, success and failure, what each action means
   and costs, what is irreversible, what the Player sees, the budget, and what feedback means. State what a competent
   decision must at least satisfy given the information at the time.
2. **Per-step reference.** For each episode compute, where possible deterministically, a reference from information the
   Player had at that step and compare it with the actual action. Hidden answers or host records may only confirm
   feedback or outcomes, never count as something the Player should have known.
3. **Decisive steps.** Find where an episode went from winnable to lost, or where the best opportunity was missed.
4. **Exclude non-capability causes first:** evidence not delivered, action-protocol rejection, interface, budget or
   environment limits, service errors, scorer confounds. List them under `nonCapabilityCauses`; they give only
   lower-confidence clues.
5. **Contrast** failing and successful episodes. A behaviour is *higher* confidence only if failing episodes show it,
   successful ones show the correct behaviour in the same situation, and non-capability causes are excluded;
   otherwise *lower*.
6. **Map to defect types.** Assign each decisive behaviour to one defect type using the exact pass / fail definitions
   of the catalogue. Separate the main cause from secondary ones. If truly no defect type fits, write
   `defectType: "NEW:<proposed name>"` with `newDefinition` and the closest existing capability; never invent a
   capability and never stretch a definition to make a behaviour fit.
7. **Task-level summary.** Count, per defect type, the failing and successful episodes showing it. A single-episode
   phenomenon is not a task-level conclusion.
8. **Failure coverage.** Every failing episode must be attributed: to a behaviour (cited), to a non-capability cause,
   or at least named in the analysis with a reason.

## Output

Finish with exactly one `submit_analysis` call. `analysis` is the Markdown report with these sections:
**Task and observation**; **Summary of findings**; **Capability gaps and where they appear** (per capability, per
defect type: (a) the behaviour, (b) occurrences with episode/step citations and outcome, (c) counts in failing and
successful episodes); **Attribution**; **What successful episodes did**; **Evidence limits**; **Episode table**;
**Failure coverage**.

The host derives the requirement set and the placement from your `behaviours` with fixed rules: the requirement
set holds every capability your behaviours evidence (missing ones from failure behaviours, satisfied ones from
success behaviours); the placement states what the failure evidence supports (missing-capability, clue, zero-failure
gap, or no gap). Success behaviours therefore matter: record the capabilities the Player exercised correctly. The host checks counts, citations, coverage and vocabulary; fix what it reports
and resubmit.
Use read_step to inspect the actual images before and after a cited action whenever the claim depends on visual evidence. Text digests contain image references, not the visual evidence itself.

Explicitly cover every ruleYardstick field in requirementCoverage. Map each applicable specification requirement to behaviourIndices, including successful H0 behaviours: already satisfied capabilities are still required. Do not omit a demand because H0 succeeds. Only use notApplicableReason when the field truly imposes no capability demand; explain why. Reviewers must reject omitted or incorrectly dismissed demands. For every behaviour, use check="run_check:<exact name>" after a successful deterministic tool execution, or check="read_steps:<reasoning>" after reading every cited step with read_step. A prose claim that a check ran is not execution evidence.
